Who I Am, On Paper

Professional Bio

Neha Vajrala is a high school senior and grassroots organizer who leads student advocacy and technology programs across her community, including as president of TSA, vice president of VEX Robotics, and founding president of her school's Georgia Youth Justice Coalition chapter. Her work includes grassroots advocacy, mentorship, and organization, grounded in the belief that lasting change is vested in the people.

Personal statement

In eighth grade, a friend asked me to join our school's robotics team. I had no experience, and it showed. I was handed the tasks nobody else wanted and treated as though I couldn't keep up. I decided to prove that assumption wrong, and spent the next few years teaching myself CAD, coding, and the strategic side of competition robotics until I was the one other students came to for help. That early experience turned into a genuine interest in biomedical engineering, particularly prosthetics, once I started thinking about my family's roots in rural India, where access to basic medical technology is still not guaranteed.

Most of my high school years have centered on technology and robotics, which isn't always what people expect given my current direction. I took over our TSA chapter when it had almost no structure or institutional memory, and rebuilt it over two years–new leadership roles, a formal onboarding process, a chapter website, and documentation so future officers wouldn't have to start from zero. As vice president of VEX Robotics, I coach younger students in CAD, coding, and competition strategy. I stayed in these clubs longer than I originally planned because I found a community that needed rebuilding, and I knew I was capable of doing it.

Outside of technology, I've directed most of my energy toward grassroots advocacy, grounded in the belief that meaningful change happens closest to the people it affects. I cofounded and lead my school's chapter of the Georgia Youth Justice Coalition, interned at the Fulton County courthouse, and serve as a Secretary of State student ambassador. Healthcare equity, civil rights, human rights, and prison reform are the issues I return to most, and I try to use whatever platform I have on behalf of people who aren't given room to speak for themselves. Across both the engineering and advocacy work, I've come to see myself as a builder–someone who never abandons a space needing rebuilding.
